# Aetherwake Visual Direction

## Art thesis

Aetherwake is a modern pixel-art JRPG, not a hardware-emulation exercise. Its visual identity combines readable 16-bit-era forms with contemporary environmental depth, restrained cinematic lighting, expressive animation, and a solarpunk/aether-tech material language.

The game must remain recognizably Aetherwake. References are studied for transferable craft principles, never copied for characters, layouts, sprites, logos, or proprietary visual motifs.

## Contemporary reference principles

- **Sea of Stars:** readable silhouettes, rich environmental motion, strong light/shadow separation, dense scenes that remain navigable.
- **Chained Echoes:** coherent top-down JRPG geography, compact world storytelling, readable traversal and settlements.
- **Octopath / HD-2D lineage:** depth staging, atmospheric separation, selective bloom-like emphasis and cinematic framing without abandoning pixel characters.
- **Eastward:** authored environmental clutter, lived-in spaces, strong local color scripts and prop storytelling.
- **CrossCode:** crisp tile readability, strong interaction language, controlled effects density.
- **Recent pixel-art RPG direction:** pixel foundations can use modern lighting, particles, camera language and high-resolution environmental composition rather than imitating a single historical console.

## Aetherwake-specific rules

1. Nature and technology interlock: vegetation grows around repaired machinery; aether systems glow as functional infrastructure rather than generic neon.
2. Use dark blue-violet structural shadows, warm weathered materials, living greens, sky cyan, and restrained luminous teal.
3. Important silhouettes must read before texture: party members, airship, settlements, ruins, enemies and traversal boundaries.
4. Detail density follows importance. Small traversal tiles stay economical; hero sprites, airship, bosses, combat scenes and key landmarks receive more pixels and animation.
5. Lighting supports hierarchy. Do not wash every surface in bloom or particles.
6. Terrain transitions should look authored: coast to shallows, grass to forest, path to settlement, mountain to snow, ruin to reclaimed nature.
7. Avoid repeated single-tile stamping as the final look. Build landmark clusters and metatiles where scale matters.
8. Effects may be modern, but their shapes should remain pixel-conscious and preserve sprite readability.
9. UI should feel like restored instrumentation: precise, compact, legible, slightly futuristic, never generic sci-fi glass.
10. Every art pass must preserve gameplay semantics unless explicitly scoped otherwise.

## Quality gate

An art change is not complete merely because it is pixelated. It should improve at least one of: silhouette, composition, material readability, environmental storytelling, animation, depth, atmosphere, hierarchy, or consistency. Regression tests protect runtime authority and gameplay boundaries.
